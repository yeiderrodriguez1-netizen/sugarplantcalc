Sucrose Supersaturation and Solubility Calculator

The supersaturation coefficient (y) is the variable that governs all crystallization in pans: it determines whether a crystal grows, dissolves, or whether unwanted spontaneous nuclei appear. This calculator computes y from the Brix, purity and temperature of a massecuite or syrup using the pure sucrose-in-water solubility correlation, and identifies which working zone it falls into.

Theoretical background and formulas

Pure sucrose solubility (Charles correlation)

\[S_{pure}(\%) = 64.397 + 0.07251\,T + 0.0020569\,T^2 - 9.035\times10^{-6}\,T^3\]

Actual sucrose/water ratio vs. saturation

\[Q_{actual} = \frac{Brix\times Purity/100}{100-Brix} \qquad Q_{sat} = \frac{S_{pure}}{100-S_{pure}}\times C_{sol}\]

Supersaturation coefficient

\[y = \frac{Q_{actual}}{Q_{sat}}\]

The solubility coefficient (\(C_{sol}\)) corrects pure sucrose solubility for the effect of the non-sugars present (lower purity reduces effective solubility), following the approach described by Rein and used in the classic pan supersaturation formula.

Reference literature: Peter Rein, Cane Sugar Engineering (supersaturation and crystallization zones); E. Hugot, Handbook of Cane Sugar Engineering; Meade–Chen, Cane Sugar Handbook (Charles solubility correlation).

Frequently asked questions

What is the metastable zone and why is it the working zone?

It is the supersaturation range (y between 1.00 and 1.20) where existing crystals grow by consuming dissolved sucrose without spontaneous new nuclei forming; that is why it is the target condition throughout boiling in the pan.

What happens if the massecuite enters the labile zone (y > 1.30)?

Uncontrolled spontaneous nucleation occurs, producing "false grain": unwanted new crystals that ruin the strike's grain size distribution and complicate later exhaustion in centrifugals.

Why does purity affect the supersaturation coefficient?

Non-sugars present in lower-purity syrups or molasses reduce the effective solubility of sucrose (solubility depression effect), so at the same Brix and temperature, a lower-purity massecuite has a higher y than a high-purity one.

What does an "undersaturated" result (y < 1.0) mean?

It indicates the solution has not reached the saturation point at that temperature and concentration: if crystals were present, they would dissolve instead of growing, so it needs to be concentrated further or cooled to enter the working zone.

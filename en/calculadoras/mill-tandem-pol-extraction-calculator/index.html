Theoretical background and formulas

Pol extraction

\[EP(\%) = 100 - \frac{Pol_{bagasse} \times Fibre_{cane}}{Fibre_{bagasse} \times Pol_{cane}} \times 100\]

Reduced extraction (normalised to 12.5% fibre — E. Hugot)

\[ER(\%) = 100 - (100-EP)\left(\frac{Fibre_{cane}}{12.5}\right)\left(\frac{87.5}{100-Fibre_{cane}}\right)\]

Imbibition % fibre

\[IF(\%) = \frac{Imbibition_{\%cane}}{Fibre_{\%cane}} \times 100\]

Expected mixed juice Brix

\[Bx_{mixed}^{expected} = Bx_{primary}\left(1-\frac{Imb}{100+Imb}\right)\]

Reference literature: E. Hugot, Handbook of Cane Sugar Engineering (reduced extraction at 12.5% fibre); Peter Rein, Cane Sugar Engineering (extraction and imbibition); Meade–Chen, Cane Sugar Handbook.

Frequently asked questions

What is a normal Pol extraction in a sugar mill?

Well-maintained tandems operate between 94% and 97%; below 94% usually indicates roll wear, insufficient imbibition or poor cane preparation.

What is the difference between Pol extraction and reduced extraction?

Reduced extraction normalises the result to 12.5% fibre in cane, allowing tandems of different varieties/seasons to be compared on equal terms (Hugot's method).

How does imbibition % fibre affect extraction?

The typical efficient range is 200–250% fibre; below it dilutes the residual juice in bagasse poorly, above it overloads evaporation without proportional extraction gain.

Why does my actual mixed juice Brix differ from the expected value?

A deviation greater than 0.8 °Bx from the value calculated by dilution usually signals leaks, poor imbibition water distribution or a sampling error.

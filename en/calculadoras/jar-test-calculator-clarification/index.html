Jar Test Calculator for Clarification

The jar test is the standard laboratory method for determining the optimal flocculant dose before adjusting it on the clarifier. This calculator converts data from up to 6 jars (dose, settling rate, mud volume and supernatant turbidity) into a weighted score and recommends the optimal dose in ppm of polymer.

How to run the test

Theoretical background and formulas

Weighted score per jar

\[Score_i = 100\times\frac{0.45\,n_{turbidity,i} + 0.35\,n_{velocity,i} + 0.20\,n_{mud,i}}{0.45+0.35+0.20\,[mud\,available]}\]

Each component is normalised against the best value in the series: \(n_{turbidity}=NTU_{min}/NTU_i\), \(n_{velocity}=v_i/v_{max}\), \(n_{mud}=mud_{min}/mud_i\) (only if there are mud readings > 0). Turbidity carries the highest weight (45%) because it is the acceptance criterion for the clarified juice; settling rate (35%) determines clarifier capacity; mud (20%) is an additional reference figure.

Test solution volume per jar

\[V_{solution}\,(mL) = \frac{Jar_{mass}\,(g) \times Dose\,(ppm)/1000}{C_{test\,solution}\,(g/L)}\]

Reference literature: Peter Rein, Cane Sugar Engineering (jar test and flocculant selection); E. Hugot, Handbook of Cane Sugar Engineering; Meade–Chen, Cane Sugar Handbook.

Frequently asked questions

Why does turbidity carry the highest weight in the score?

Because it is the direct acceptance criterion for clarified juice going to evaporation: lower supernatant turbidity indicates better removal of suspended solids, hence its 45% weight versus 35% for settling rate and 20% for mud.

What does it mean when the optimal dose falls at the edge of the tested series?

If the winning jar is the highest or lowest dose tested, the true optimum could lie outside the evaluated range; the calculator recommends repeating the test expanding the dose range in that direction.

When should mud volume be read: at minute 1 or at 30 minutes?

Mud volume is read at 30 minutes, once settling has finished, as an additional reference figure; settling rate (used in the score) is measured at minute 1 by reading the interface drop.

What if I only have turbidity and dose, without a mud reading?

The score is recalculated using only turbidity (45%) and velocity (35%) normalised to 100%, without penalising the absence of mud data; mud is additional information, not mandatory to get a recommendation.

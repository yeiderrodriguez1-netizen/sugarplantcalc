Theoretical background and formulas

Factory yield

\[Yield(\%) = \frac{Sugar_{produced}}{Cane_{milled}} \times 100\]

Sugar per cane

\[kg_{sugar}/t_{cane} = \frac{Sugar_{produced}\,(t) \times 1000}{Cane_{milled}\,(t)}\]

Overall recovery

\[Recovery(\%) = \frac{Factory_{yield}}{Pol_{\%cane}} \times 100\]

Overall recovery expresses what fraction of the sucrose that entered with the cane was actually converted into commercial sugar, combining milling, clarification, evaporation and crystallization losses into a single number (Meade–Chen).

Reference literature: Meade–Chen, Cane Sugar Handbook (factory yield and overall recovery); Peter Rein, Cane Sugar Engineering; E. Hugot, Handbook of Cane Sugar Engineering.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good factory yield in a sugar mill?

It depends on variety and region, but values between 10% and 13% are typical; below 10% usually points to low-pol cane or high process losses.

Why does the calculator say the result is impossible?

If the calculated factory yield exceeds the cane's Pol %, it would mean recovering more sugar than the sucrose the cane actually carried, which cannot happen: check the units of sugar produced and cane milled.

What is the difference between factory yield and overall recovery?

Factory yield is sugar produced over cane milled; overall recovery normalises it by dividing by the cane's Pol %, allowing comparison of seasons or mills with cane of different quality.

What factors lower factory yield the most?

The main ones are low milling extraction, sucrose losses in filter mud and molasses, and sucrose inversion from prolonged process times or inadequate pH.

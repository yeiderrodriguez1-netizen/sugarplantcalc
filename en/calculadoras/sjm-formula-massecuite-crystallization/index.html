Theoretical background and formulas

S-J-M yield

\[SJM(\%) = \frac{Pu_{crystal}\,(Pu_{liquor}-Pu_{motherliquor})}{Pu_{liquor}\,(Pu_{crystal}-Pu_{motherliquor})}\times100\]

% crystal in massecuite

\[\%Crystal = \frac{Bx_{massecuite}\,(Pu_{massecuite}-Pu_{motherliquor})}{100-Pu_{motherliquor}}\]

Pan mass balance

\[m_{massecuite} = V \times \rho \qquad m_{liquor} = m_{massecuite}\times\frac{Bx_{massecuite}}{Bx_{liquor}} \qquad m_{water\,evap} = m_{liquor}-m_{massecuite}\]

Reference literature: Peter Rein, Cane Sugar Engineering (crystallization and pan mass balance); E. Hugot, Handbook of Cane Sugar Engineering; Meade–Chen, Cane Sugar Handbook (purity drop methods).

Frequently asked questions

What does S-J-M mean in sugar crystallization?

It identifies the three purities in the balance: Sucrose (crystal), Juice (feed liquor) and Molasses (mother liquor). Also known as the purity drop formula.

What is the ideal % crystal in an A massecuite?

Typically between 40% and 55%. Below 30% it is considered low crystal content.

How does mother liquor purity relate to exhaustion?

The mother liquor must have lower purity than the massecuite and the feed liquor. If that relationship does not hold, the data is inconsistent.

What is the supersaturation coefficient and how is it used here?

It is a factor derived from sucrose solubility at the working temperature, calculated in detail in the Solubility calculator and used here as an input to the balance.

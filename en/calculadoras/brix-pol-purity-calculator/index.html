Purity Calculator from Brix and Pol

Purity is the ratio between sucrose (Pol) and total soluble solids (Brix) in a juice, syrup or molasses. It is the most widely used lab indicator for tracking extraction, clarification and evaporation quality in a sugar mill. This calculator computes apparent purity and flags readings that are low or physically impossible.

Theoretical background and formulas

Apparent purity

\[Purity(\%) = \frac{Pol}{Brix} \times 100\]

Apparent purity assumes refractometric Brix represents total soluble solids and Pol represents apparent sucrose measured by polarimetry. It is a working approximation (not true purity from HPLC), but it is the daily factory-control standard (Meade–Chen).

Reference literature: Meade–Chen, Cane Sugar Handbook (apparent purity definition and typical purities by stream); Peter Rein, Cane Sugar Engineering; E. Hugot, Handbook of Cane Sugar Engineering.

Frequently asked questions

What does it mean if juice purity is low?

Purity below 70% usually indicates deteriorated cane, contamination with non-sugars, or a sampling error; the normal range for mixed juice is 78–88%.

Why does the calculator flag "impossible purity" in some cases?

If the Pol reading is greater than Brix, the formula gives over 100%, which is not physically possible because sucrose cannot exceed total dissolved solids: check the refractometer and polarimeter.

What is the difference between apparent purity and true purity?

Apparent purity uses refractometric Brix and polarized Pol, which include other dissolved solids; true purity requires actual sucrose analysis (HPLC) and is usually slightly different.

How does purity change along the factory process?

It generally rises slightly from mixed juice to syrup due to clarification (removal of non-sugars), then falls in the molasses as crystallizable sucrose is exhausted.

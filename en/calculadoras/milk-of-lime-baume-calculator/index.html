Milk of Lime and Baumé Degrees Calculator

Milk of lime is prepared by slaking quicklime (CaO) in water to a concentration expressed in Baumé degrees, and is dosed into the juice to neutralise acidity in the liming stage. This calculator computes the milk of lime flow to the juice, the valve setting, the commercial quicklime required per day, and the approximate slaking water, from the CaO demand and milk of lime concentration.

Theoretical background and formulas

Milk of lime density from Baumé degrees

\[SG_{milk} = \frac{145}{145-°Bé}\]

Required CaO and milk of lime flow

\[CaO_{kg/h} = Q_{juice}\,(m^3/h) \times Demand\,(kg\,CaO/m^3)\]
\[Q_{milk}\,(L/h) = \frac{CaO_{kg/h} \times 1000}{g_{CaO}\,(g/L\,milk)}\]

Commercial quicklime

\[Quicklime_{commercial} = \frac{CaO_{kg/h}}{(\%Purity/100)\times(\%Slaking_{efficiency}/100)}\]

The 0.7569 factor converts Ca(OH)₂ to equivalent CaO (molar mass ratio 56.08/74.09), and 2.24 kg/L is the reference density of pure Ca(OH)₂ used to estimate dissolved solids in the milk of lime from its specific gravity (Hugot).

Reference literature: E. Hugot, Handbook of Cane Sugar Engineering (liming and milk of lime preparation); Peter Rein, Cane Sugar Engineering; Meade–Chen, Cane Sugar Handbook.

Frequently asked questions

Why is milk of lime handled in Baumé degrees instead of Brix?

Baumé degrees can be read directly with a hydrometer without calculating composition, and the scale is calibrated for denser suspensions like milk of lime, unlike Brix which is specific to sugar solutions.

What happens if the milk of lime is above 15 °Bé?

A milk of lime that is too concentrated settles easily and clogs lines, valves and dosing nozzles; the recommended operating range is 6–12 °Bé to maintain good flowability.

How does CaO demand relate to cane quality?

More deteriorated cane or juice with higher natural acidity requires more lime to reach the neutralisation pH; demands below 0.5 kg CaO/t cane usually leave the juice insufficiently neutralised.

Why is the factor 0.7569 used in the quicklime calculation?

It is the molar mass ratio between CaO (56.08 g/mol) and Ca(OH)₂ (74.09 g/mol); it converts the equivalent Ca(OH)₂ present in the milk of lime into the amount of CaO (quicklime) needed to produce it.

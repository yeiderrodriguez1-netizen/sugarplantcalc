Theoretical background and formulas

Water evaporated and syrup produced

\[\%Evaporation = \left(1-\frac{Bx_{clarified}}{Bx_{syrup,target}}\right)\times100\]
\[Water_{evaporated}\,(t/h) = Clarified_{juice}\,(t/h) \times \frac{\%Evaporation}{100}\]

Steam economy

\[Economy\,(kg/kg) = \frac{Water_{evaporated}}{Steam_{fed\,1st\,effect}}\]

Specific evaporation rate

\[Rate\,(kg/m^2\cdot h) = \frac{Water_{evaporated}\times1000}{Calandria_{area}}\]

Typical steam economy scales roughly with the number of effects (Hugot): 2.6 kg/kg in triple effect, 3.2 kg/kg in quadruple, and 4.0 kg/kg in quintuple effect; these values are used as a reference to estimate theoretical syrup Brix and detect deviations against actual Brix.

Reference literature: E. Hugot, Handbook of Cane Sugar Engineering (multiple effect evaporation and steam economy); Peter Rein, Cane Sugar Engineering; Meade–Chen, Cane Sugar Handbook.

Frequently asked questions

What steam economy should I expect in a quadruple effect evaporator?

A typical reference value is 3.2 kg of water evaporated per kg of steam fed to the first effect; below that, check for calandria scaling or insufficient vapour bleeding.

Why is very concentrated syrup (>70 °Bx) a risk?

At very high concentrations, syrup can start crystallising spontaneously inside the evaporator itself or in outlet piping, causing plugging and loss of control over downstream crystallization.

How is theoretical syrup Brix calculated and what is it used for?

It is estimated from the steam fed and the typical steam economy for the number of effects; compared with the measured actual Brix, a deviation greater than 2 points usually indicates an instrumentation error (refractometer) or steam flow measurement error.

What effect does low condenser vacuum have on evaporation?

A vacuum below 24 inHg in the last effect reduces the available temperature differential for heat transfer, decreasing the evaporation capacity of the entire train, even if inlet steam is sufficient.

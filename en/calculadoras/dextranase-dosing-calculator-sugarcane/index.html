Dextranase Dosing Calculator for Sugarcane Juice

Dextranase hydrolyses the dextran formed by bacteria (Leuconostoc) in deteriorated or long-stored cane, preventing viscosity problems, crystal elongation and false grain in crystallization. This calculator computes enzyme consumption in g/h and kg/day, and the diluted dosing pump flow, from juice flow, measured dextran, target dose, and temperature/retention time conditions.

Theoretical background and formulas

Enzyme consumption

\[Enzyme_{g/h} = Juice_{flow}\,(t/h) \times Target_{dose}\,(ppm)\]

Dosing pump flow (diluted enzyme)

\[Q_{pump}\,(L/h) = \frac{Enzyme_{g/h}/\rho_{enzyme}}{1000 \times (Dilution_{\%}/100)}\]

Effective dextranase dosing depends critically on temperature and retention time in the reaction tank: outside its optimum window, the enzyme becomes inactive or fails to hydrolyse the dextran before the juice moves further in the process (Eggleston, ISSCT).

Reference literature: Peter Rein, Cane Sugar Engineering (dextran and its enzymatic control); E. Hugot, Handbook of Cane Sugar Engineering; Meade–Chen, Cane Sugar Handbook (cane deterioration and dextran).

Frequently asked questions

What level of dextran in juice is considered severe?

Above 500 ppm is considered severe dextran, with high risk of increased viscosity, needle-like crystal elongation and false grain in the pans.

What is the optimal temperature for dosing dextranase?

The typical working range is 45–65 °C; above 65 °C the enzyme becomes thermally inactivated, and below 45 °C its kinetics are too slow to hydrolyse the dextran in time.

Why does retention time matter in addition to dose?

The enzyme needs at least 10 minutes of contact with the juice to effectively hydrolyse the dextran; a correct dose with insufficient retention time will not achieve the expected effect.

What does the "double failure" alert mean?

It appears when temperature is out of the optimal range AND retention time is low at the same time, indicating the enzyme is likely not effective at all, not just partially.

Theoretical background and formulas

P₂O₅ deficit and required addition

\[P_2O_{5\,to\,add}\,(kg/h) = \frac{\dot{m}_{juice}\,(kg/h) \times (P_2O_{5\,target} - P_2O_{5\,natural})\,(ppm)}{10^6 \times \eta_{dosing}}\]

Equivalent pure H₃PO₄

\[H_3PO_{4\,pure} = \frac{P_2O_{5\,to\,add}}{0.7243}\]

Commercial acid and pump flow

\[\dot{m}_{commercial\,acid} = \frac{H_3PO_{4\,pure}}{\%H_3PO_4/100} \qquad Q_{L/h} = \frac{\dot{m}_{commercial\,acid}}{\rho_{acid}}\]

The 0.7243 factor is the stoichiometric molar mass ratio between P₂O₅ (141.94 g/mol) and H₃PO₄ (2 × 98 g/mol), used to convert elemental P₂O₅ into equivalent pure acid (Hugot).

Reference literature: E. Hugot, Handbook of Cane Sugar Engineering (phosphatation and clarification); Peter Rein, Cane Sugar Engineering; Meade–Chen, Cane Sugar Handbook.

Frequently asked questions

What is the typical target P₂O₅ range for good juice clarification?

The usual range is 250–350 ppm P₂O₅ in juice; below 250 ppm phosphatation defecation usually loses effectiveness in forming calcium phosphate floc.

Why is dosing above 400 ppm P₂O₅ not recommended?

Excess phosphate above 400 ppm unnecessarily raises lime consumption to neutralise the acid and increases the volume of filter mud generated, without a proportional clarification gain.

What does it mean that the juice "already covers the target"?

Some canes naturally have high P₂O₅; if the measured natural P₂O₅ already equals or exceeds the process target, no additional phosphoric acid dosing is needed.

Why is the factor 0.7243 used in the calculation?

It is the molar mass ratio between P₂O₅ (141.94 g/mol) and H₃PO₄ (2 × 97.99 g/mol); it converts the required elemental P₂O₅ mass into the equivalent mass of pure phosphoric acid.

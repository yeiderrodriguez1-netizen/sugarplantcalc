Theoretical background and formulas

Pure polymer required

\[Polymer_{kg/h} = \dot{m}_{juice}\,(kg/h) \times \frac{Dose_{ppm}}{10^6}\]

Solution pump flow

\[Q_{pump}\,(L/h) = \frac{Polymer_{kg/h} \times 1000}{C_{stock\,solution}\,(g/L)}\]

Batch duration

\[t_{batch}\,(h) = \frac{V_{tank}\,(m^3) \times 1000}{Q_{pump}\,(L/h)}\]

The polymer stock solution is prepared dilute (typically 0.5–1.5 g/L) because at higher concentration its viscosity rises exponentially, hindering both complete dissolution and uniform dosing (Rein).

Reference literature: Peter Rein, Cane Sugar Engineering (flocculants and clarification); E. Hugot, Handbook of Cane Sugar Engineering; Meade–Chen, Cane Sugar Handbook.

Frequently asked questions

What is the typical flocculant dose in cane juice clarification?

The usual range is 1–6 ppm of polymer on the juice; below 1 ppm a stable floc rarely forms, and above 6 ppm it is a typical overdose that can reflocculate the mud and increase turbidity.

Why should the stock solution not be prepared too concentrated?

Above 2 g/L the viscosity of the polyacrylamide solution rises sharply, hindering complete dissolution and uniform dosing; the recommended practical range is 0.5–1.5 g/L.

What happens if the batch lasts less than 1.5 hours?

The polymer needs a 30–60 minute ageing time after dissolving to develop its full chain and effectiveness; a batch consumed in less than 1.5 hours does not give enough time, so a larger tank or two alternating tanks should be used.

How does the flocculant dose relate to the jar test?

The optimal ppm dose obtained from a laboratory jar test is the ideal starting point for this calculator, since it field-validates the working range before adjusting the plant dosing pump.

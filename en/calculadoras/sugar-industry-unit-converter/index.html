Theoretical background and formulas

Linear factor conversion

\[V_{target} = V_{source} \times \frac{f_{source}}{f_{target}}\]

Each unit within a category (e.g. kPa, bar, psi, kg/cm² and mmHg for pressure) has a conversion factor relative to a base unit. The input value is first brought to the base unit and then distributed to every other unit in the group.

Temperature conversion

\[°C = (°F - 32) \times \frac{5}{9} \qquad °F = °C \times \frac{9}{5} + 32\]

Concentration conversion (with density)

\[ppm = \%w/w \times 10000 \qquad ppm = \frac{mg/L}{\rho_{solution}}\]

Converting between mg/L and ppm requires the solution density because ppm is a mass ratio (mg of solute per kg of solution) while mg/L is mass per volume.

Reference literature: E. Hugot, Handbook of Cane Sugar Engineering (units and conversion factors for industrial use); Peter Rein, Cane Sugar Engineering; Meade–Chen, Cane Sugar Handbook.

Frequently asked questions

Why does converting mg/L to ppm require the solution density?

Because ppm is a mass fraction (mg of solute per kg of solution) while mg/L is mass per volume; they are only numerically equal when the solution density is 1.00 kg/L, as in dilute water.

Which pressure units are most used in a sugar mill?

kPa and bar for modern instrumentation, psi on US-origin equipment, kg/cm² on older gauges, and mmHg or inHg specifically for vacuum readings in evaporators and pans.

How do you convert HP to kW for mill drives and turbines?

Multiply the power in HP by the factor 0.7456999 to get kilowatts; it is the standard conversion used when comparing mill drives, turbogenerators and pumps.

What is the difference between viscosity in cP and Pa·s?

1 Pa·s equals 1000 cP; centipoise (cP) is the practical reference unit for molasses and massecuites because water at 20 °C has approximately 1 cP.
